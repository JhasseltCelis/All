The short version

The page is disciplined. It uses one smooth curve, respects people who turn motion off, and avoids the usual bouncing and fading clutter. The problem is where the motion is spent. The biggest moments, opening the interview and sending the booking form, have no motion at all and simply snap. Meanwhile small, repeated things like chat messages move slowly, and a fake "live" light blinks forever. Move the effort to the moments that matter.

How each lens reads the motion

Three viewpoints, weighted for a marketing page. Production polish leads, playful ideas come second, and the speed and restraint view is used for the forms, the navigation, and the chat.

Where the timings land

Every animation has a length, measured in milliseconds (1000ms is one second). Short is right for things that happen often. Longer is fine for big, rare moments. Each dot is one animation on the page today. The hollow dots are changes that happen with no animation at all.

Instant and responsive, 0 to 300ms. Where nearly everything belongs: taps, hovers, buttons.
Deliberate, 300 to 500ms. Earned by large surfaces and rare moments.

What's off

The budget is upside down. The headline (1) is rightly slow because it plays once. But chat messages (2) are the most repeated motion and sit in the deliberate zone, while the five biggest changes (8 to 12) get no motion at all.

CriticalJakub

Opening the interview is a hard cut

What's wrongClicking "Interview" or "Try the interview" swaps the whole page in a single frame and jumps to the top. "Back to home" does the same in reverse.

Why it mattersIt feels like a different site loaded, or that something broke, right at the step you most want people to take.

The fixFade the old view out quickly and let the new one rise in gently. Skip it for people who turn motion off.

For your developerWrap the swap in route() with document.startViewTransition(). Old view: opacity to 0, 160ms ease-out. New view: opacity 0 to 1 and translateY(12px) to 0, 280ms cubic-bezier(0.16, 1, 0.3, 1). Keep the instant swap when the API is missing or reduced motion is on.

Old content replays its entrance every time it comes back

What's wrongHidden sections restart their animations when shown again. Coming back from the interview replays the big headline rise. Going back to the interview, or reloading mid-interview, makes every past message float up at once.

Why it mattersEntrances are for new things. When old messages jump, it looks like the chat reset, which worries people who were halfway through.

The fixPlay each entrance once. Messages that are already there just sit still. Only the newest message moves.

For your developerIn the resume block, add a class such as .msg.restored { animation: none } to rebuilt bubbles. For the hero, after the first animationend, set a class on <html> that turns off .hero h1 span animation, so un-hiding #view-home does not replay it.

Issues to address

ImportantEmil

The "live" light on the chat preview blinks forever

What's wrongThe blue square next to "live" in the How it works preview blinks every 1.6 seconds, forever. The preview itself is a still picture.

Why it mattersA blinking "live" light on something that isn't live is a telltale sign of a template site. It also pulls the eye away from the messages, which are the real selling point.

The fixStop the blink and keep the square solid. Better still, let the preview play its three messages once when it comes into view, then rest.

For your developerRemove the animation on .live i. For the play-once version: IntersectionObserver, each bubble opacity 0 to 1 and translateY(6px) to 0, 240ms var(--ease), 700ms apart, once, skipped under reduced motion.

The scrolling logo strip can't be stopped on phones or by keyboard

What's wrongThe black band of tool names scrolls forever. It only pauses when a mouse hovers over it.

Why it mattersText that never stops moving is hard to read and can cause discomfort. Accessibility guidelines (WCAG 2.2.2) ask for a way to pause anything that moves for more than 5 seconds.

The fixAdd a small pause button. Also pause it when it is off screen, and when it is tapped or focused.

For your developerA <button aria-pressed> that toggles animation-play-state: paused; also pause on .band:focus-within and when an IntersectionObserver reports it out of view. Keep the 38s linear speed.

Every chat message floats up slowly, even your own

What's wrongEach message rises 18px over 400ms. That includes the visitor's own answer, sent by pressing Enter.

Why it mattersAn interview has a dozen or more messages. A slow float on each one feels sluggish, and your own words should land the instant you send them.

The fixMake your own messages appear almost instantly. Keep a shorter, smaller rise for the interviewer's replies.

For your developer.msg.me: opacity and translateY(4px), 140ms ease-out. .msg.ai: opacity and translateY(8px), 260ms var(--ease). .msg.sys and .msg.err: opacity only, 160ms.

Which lens mattered most

Production polish (Jakub Krehel) shaped most of this review.

A marketing page lives or dies on trust. Most of the problems are seams: places where one screen becomes another with no care. Polish fixes those without making the page busier.

  • Lean on EmilCut motion further: drop the chat rise entirely, keep the headline but halve it to 450ms, and make the logo strip static by default.
  • Lean on JakubFix the five hollow dots first, then unify every transition on --ease at 160 to 320ms.
  • Lean on JheyBuild the before and after switch and the playing chat preview. Let those two carry the page's personality.
